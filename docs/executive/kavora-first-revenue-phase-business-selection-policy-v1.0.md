## Primary Decision Rule
KAVORA's highest-order Business Selection criterion is future net-profit potential.

## Operating Sequence
Find proven money flow → verify multiple independent sellers → analyze winners → reproduce the business mechanism lawfully → test sale before build → fulfill only what sells → automate/scale proven work.

## Proven Demand Rule
KAVORA does not default to 0→1 product ideation in the First Revenue Phase.

Preferred evidence:
- multiple independent sellers with real commercial activity;
- a meaningful winner or group of winners;
- repeated buyer demand, repeat purchase, subscription, or recurring contracts where observable;
- evidence of actual purchase/contract behavior rather than listing existence alone.

Not sufficient by itself:
- AI inference that a product is useful;
- listing count;
- views, likes, followers, or market-size articles;
- a single exceptional success story without broader market evidence.

## Winner Analysis Rule
KAVORA may analyze public winner patterns including buyer, problem/desire, offer, price, acquisition, delivery, repeat/retention, unit economics, and operating design.

Reproduction must remain within legal, contractual, platform, privacy, and intellectual-property boundaries. Do not copy protected text, images, branding, code, identity, reviews, or other protected assets.

## Owner Boundary
- Owner face/body required: NOT ALLOWED.
- Low-paid labor-intensive business: NOT ALLOWED.
- Owner identity: use brand/KAVORA publicly; Owner real identity only where legitimately required for KYC, contract, payment, or equivalent back-office requirements.
- Customer communication: allowed when KAVORA/AI/other operators can handle it.
- Revenue model: unrestricted if lawful and economically attractive.
- B2B/B2C: unrestricted.
- Industry/category: unrestricted by default, including business-improvement offers, provided demand and economics are proven.
- Adult/high-sensitivity categories: may be considered when lawful, platform-compliant, and risk-manageable.
- Acquisition channel: unrestricted if lawful and platform-compliant.
- Platform dependence: acceptable if economically attractive.
- Geography: choose the market with the strongest economic opportunity.
- Brand structure: may vary by business.
- Fulfillment: AI and outsourcing may be used.
- Owner work: important/high-value Owner work is acceptable when economics justify it; low-value routine labor should not be pushed back to Owner.

## Funding Boundary
Until revenue is produced, initial spend should normally be approximately zero.
- Free setup: allowed when useful.
- Owner-funded spend: requires Owner Decision.
- Inventory-heavy business: defer until revenue/economic evidence justifies it.
- Reinvestment after revenue: no fixed ratio; decide case-by-case with Owner living-expense needs and ROI in view.

## First-Sale and Scaling Rule
- Prefer candidates where sale potential can be tested within days to roughly one week.
- First sale may be low-ticket if it provides real demand evidence without becoming low-paid labor.
- Initial testing may run 2–3 candidates in parallel; more may be tested when funding, Owner burden, and execution cost remain near zero.
- After first sale, prefer mechanisms that can repeat, recur, subscribe, or scale.
- Proven winners should be scaled and multiple profitable businesses may accumulate over time.

## Competition Rule
Competition is not a disqualifier. A competitive market can be positive demand evidence when buyer money is substantial, multiple independent sellers are viable, new entrants have a plausible path, competition is not purely a race to the bottom, and KAVORA can reproduce or improve economics.

## What KAVORA Will Not Do by Default
- Generate generic AI product ideas, build them, list them, and wait.
- Treat “AI can make this” as demand evidence.
- Treat passive marketplace listing as a complete Revenue Engine.
- Build large automation/governance infrastructure before a real revenue bottleneck requires it.
- Preserve a strategy merely because work has already been invested in it.

## Execution Sequence
1. Find proven money flows.
2. Verify multiple independent sellers and at least one meaningful winner.
3. Decompose the winner business mechanism.
4. Remove candidates that violate Owner boundaries or cannot plausibly produce attractive net profit.
5. Reduce to a small number of candidates.
6. Test the offer before building substantial fulfillment infrastructure.
7. Build/fulfill only after buyer evidence warrants it.
8. Automate only the real bottlenecks of a proven Revenue Loop.
9. Continue, revise, stop, or scale based on external revenue evidence and economics.

## Current Non-Decision
This Policy does not select a specific business, product, service, market, or winner. Specific Business Selection remains pending external market evidence.
