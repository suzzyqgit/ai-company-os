# KAVORA Recent Executive Learning v1.0

## Purpose

Preserve recent CEO-level learning that materially improves KAVORA's customer evidence acquisition, market-scope discipline, revenue progression, and executive decision calibration.

This artifact preserves durable learning and approved direction. It does not preserve target-level outreach details, contact information, temporary execution counts, channel limits, gate timing, or individual response status. It does not amend Architecture, Governance, Role Profiles, Project Instructions, or Standards.

## 1. Customer Evidence Acquisition Learning

- Candidate 002 Batch 1 produced negative recruitment evidence: 20 of 20 cold-email outreaches were executed and no reply was confirmed at the 48-hour gate.
- The post-execution target audit classified 16 targets as language matched, 2 as mismatched, and 2 as unverified. No English-language send to a Japanese-primary target was confirmed.
- A zero-reply result is evidence about the tested recruitment method and audience access. It does not, by itself, prove that the underlying customer problem does not exist.
- When target evidence is scarce, recruitment-channel fit is part of the validation design. KAVORA should reach prospects where they are demonstrably active and begin with a low-friction, one-question interaction before requesting a larger response.
- Continued validation must not repeat a failed recruitment method without a material change supported by evidence.

## 2. Market Scope Learning

- Language Fit and Market Scope are separate decisions. The language used for outreach or content does not, by itself, establish the market being validated.
- Finding prospects through global platforms does not make the validation market global.
- Market Scope Drift occurs when recruitment or evidence collection expands beyond the explicitly selected market without a corresponding decision.
- Evidence must retain its market context. Evidence from another market may support comparison or hypothesis formation, but it cannot be silently combined with evidence for the selected market's promotion gate.

## 3. Candidate 002 Current Scoped Market Decision

- Japan is the primary validation market for Candidate 002.
- Global evidence is supporting and comparative only.
- Global evidence may inform the problem hypothesis and future market decisions, but it must not be aggregated into a Japan-specific promotion gate.
- Candidate 002 remains in problem validation. Product, Solution, Pricing, and Development are not authorized by this scoped market decision.
- Progression requires Japan-specific evidence sufficient for the applicable validation gate or a new explicit market-scope decision.

## 4. KAVORA Revenue Thesis

KAVORA's current revenue thesis follows this evidence sequence:

`Paid human + AI resolution -> Payment Evidence -> Repeatability -> Standardization -> Software / Automation -> Recurring high-margin revenue`

- Begin by resolving a real customer problem through a paid human-and-AI-assisted service when that is the fastest credible path to revenue evidence.
- Treat payment as stronger evidence than interest, positive feedback, or activity completion.
- Confirm that the result can be repeated before standardizing the delivery method.
- Introduce software and automation after repeatability is demonstrated and when they improve margin, capacity, reliability, or Owner leverage.
- Recurring high-margin revenue is the intended destination of the sequence, not a status that may be asserted before supporting revenue evidence exists.

## 5. CEO Management Learning

- Executive activity must remain connected to a credible path toward revenue, cost reduction, competitive advantage, or a decision that changes resource allocation.
- Work Progress, Evidence Progress, and Revenue Progress are distinct:
  - **Work Progress** records completed execution, preparation, and artifacts.
  - **Evidence Progress** records validated customer, market, problem, and decision evidence.
  - **Revenue Progress** records commercial outcomes such as payment and realized profit.
- Progress in one category must not be reported as progress in another. High activity does not establish Revenue Progress, and learning does not establish payment.
- Management review should identify which next action can most directly improve evidence quality or move KAVORA toward actual revenue while protecting the Owner's constraints and current priority.

## 6. CEO Decision Calibration Learning Practice

Use the following learning loop for material CEO decisions:

`Decision -> Prediction -> Evidence -> Error -> Learning -> Next Decision`

- Record the decision and the expected observable result clearly enough to compare with later evidence.
- Compare the prediction with actual evidence rather than evaluating the decision from activity volume or narrative plausibility.
- Identify the gap between prediction and evidence without converting inference into fact.
- Preserve the durable learning that changes future judgment, while leaving temporary operational state outside long-term knowledge.
- Apply the learning to the next decision and repeat the loop.

This practice improves decision calibration without creating a new governance layer, standard, framework, or KPI.
